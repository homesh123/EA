📊 Averages and spread: mean, median, variance

EnglishHinglish

🏠 Real-life example

Nine friends earn ₹30,000 a month and one earns ₹10 lakh. The 'average' (mean) salary comes out above ₹1.2 lakh, which describes nobody! The median, the middle person's salary, is ₹30,000 and tells the real story. And 'spread' asks whether everyone earns about the same or the numbers are all over the place.

💡 In simple words

Before modelling, you describe your data. The mean is the sum divided by the count, and it's pulled around by extreme values (outliers). The median is the middle value after sorting, and it's robust to outliers. The mode is the most common value. Spread measures how scattered the numbers are: variance is the average squared distance from the mean, and standard deviation is its square root, in the same units as the data. Always check both the centre and the spread.

🔑 Key terms

Mean
The usual average: sum ÷ count.
Median
The middle value when sorted. Not fooled by outliers.
Standard deviation
The typical distance of values from the mean.

👀 See it in action

python
import statistics as st
salaries = [30, 30, 30, 30, 30, 30, 30, 30, 30, 1000]  # in ₹ thousand
print(st.mean(salaries))    # 127.0  ← misleading
print(st.median(salaries))  # 30.0   ← the real picture
print(round(st.stdev(salaries), 1))  # 306.7 ← huge spread

One outlier changes the mean a lot but barely moves the median.

✅ Quick check

House prices in a city include a few very expensive villas. Which describes a typical house better?

  1. Mean
  2. Median
  3. Maximum
Show answer

B. Median: A few villas pull the mean up. The median stays at the middle house.

📌 Remember

Use the median when there are outliers, and always look at the spread too.
