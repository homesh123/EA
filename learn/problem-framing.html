🎯 Problem framing: should AI solve this, and how?

EnglishHinglish

🏠 Real-life example

A patient walks into a clinic and says 'give me antibiotics'. A good doctor first asks what the symptoms are, since when, and what has been tried. Often the real need is different from the request. When a client says 'we need an AI chatbot', a good AI professional diagnoses before prescribing.

💡 In simple words

Start from the problem, not the technology. Ask who has the problem, how often, and what it costs today. Define success as a measurable metric ('cut average ticket handling time from 9 to 5 minutes'). Check whether the data and workflow exist to support a solution. Then pick the simplest approach that works, which might be a rule, a dashboard, a classic ML model, an LLM feature or an agent. AI is a good fit when the task is frequent, pattern-based, tolerant of occasional errors and backed by data. It's a poor fit when every mistake is costly and rules already work.

🔑 Key terms

Success metric
A number that shows whether the solution worked.
Baseline
How things perform today, without the new solution.
Simplest viable approach
The least complex solution that meets the goal.

👀 See it in action

text
Ask: "Build an AI chatbot for our support team"
Discovery: 60% of tickets are 'where is my order?'
Baseline:  9 min average handling, 30k tickets/month
Better fix: auto-reply with live order status (API) + LLM drafts for the rest
Metric:    handling time 9 → 5 min, CSAT ≥ 4.3

Diagnosis changed the solution, and made it cheaper and more effective.

✅ Quick check

A client asks for 'AI to stop customers leaving'. What's the best first step?

  1. Train a deep learning model right away
  2. Define 'leaving', measure the baseline and check what data exists
  3. Build a chatbot
Show answer

B. Define 'leaving', measure the baseline and check what data exists: Without a clear definition, baseline and data, any model is a guess.

📌 Remember

Problem → metric → data → simplest solution. Diagnose before you prescribe.
