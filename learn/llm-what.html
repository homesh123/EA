💬 What is an LLM? Tokens, context and temperature

EnglishHinglish

🏠 Real-life example

Your phone keyboard suggests the next word as you type. An LLM (large language model) is that idea scaled up enormously: trained on a huge share of the internet's text, it predicts the next word so well that whole essays, code and answers come out. The 'context window' is how much of the conversation it can keep in mind at once, like the size of a whiteboard.

💡 In simple words

LLMs read and write in tokens, chunks of text roughly ¾ of an English word. Hindi and other languages often use more tokens per word. You pay for, and are limited by, tokens. The context window is the maximum number of tokens (your prompt plus documents plus the answer) the model can handle at once. Temperature controls randomness: low (0–0.3) gives focused, repeatable answers, and higher values are more creative. LLMs don't 'look things up' unless you give them tools or documents. They generate likely text, which is why they can confidently be wrong (hallucinate).

🔑 Key terms

Token
The unit LLMs read and bill by, a word-piece.
Context window
The maximum text the model can consider in one go.
Hallucination
A confident-sounding answer that is made up or wrong.

👀 See it in action

text
Prompt: "Suggest a name for my chai startup"
temperature 0.0 → "Chai Point" (same every time)
temperature 1.0 → "Kettle & Kulhad", "Brew Bandhu", "Sip Sutra"… (varies)

Tokens: "Suggest a name for my chai startup" ≈ 8 tokens

Low temperature for facts and code, higher for brainstorming.

✅ Quick check

Why can an LLM state a wrong fact confidently?

  1. It is lying on purpose
  2. It generates likely-sounding text and doesn't check facts by itself
  3. Its internet is slow
Show answer

B. It generates likely-sounding text and doesn't check facts by itself: LLMs predict plausible text. Without retrieval or tools they have no fact-checking step.

📌 Remember

LLM = super-powered next-word predictor. Mind tokens, context and temperature.
