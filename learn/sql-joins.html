🧩 SQL JOINs: combining tables

EnglishHinglish

🏠 Real-life example

Your school kept one register for student names and roll numbers, and another for marks by roll number. To print 'name + marks', you match the two registers on roll number. A JOIN does that matching for database tables.

💡 In simple words

Databases split data into separate tables to avoid repetition, such as customers and orders, linked by a key like customer_id. A JOIN brings them back together. An INNER JOIN keeps only rows that match in both tables. A LEFT JOIN keeps every row from the left table, with blanks where there's no match, which is perfect for questions like 'customers who never ordered'. Joining on the wrong key, or getting duplicate matches, is the most common source of wrong numbers.

🎬 See how it works

INNER JOIN keeps only customers who have orders (the overlap). LEFT JOIN keeps every customer, with or without orders.

🔑 Key terms

Primary / foreign key
An ID that uniquely identifies a row, and the column in another table that points to it.
INNER JOIN
Only rows that match in both tables.
LEFT JOIN
All rows from the left table, plus matches from the right if there are any.

👀 See it in action

sql
-- Customers who have never placed an order
SELECT c.name
FROM customers c
LEFT JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.id
WHERE o.id IS NULL;

A LEFT JOIN plus 'IS NULL' finds the rows with no match.

✅ Quick check

You need every product, including ones that never sold. Which join?

  1. LEFT JOIN from products to sales
  2. INNER JOIN products with sales
  3. No join is needed
Show answer

A. LEFT JOIN from products to sales: A LEFT JOIN keeps all products even when no sales row matches. An INNER JOIN would drop them.

📌 Remember

INNER = only matches. LEFT = keep everything on the left.
