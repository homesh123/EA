🗣️ Explaining AI to non-technical people

EnglishHinglish

🏠 Real-life example

A good CA doesn't explain tax law section by section to a shop owner. They say: 'If you do X, you save ₹40,000 this year, and here's the one risk.' Explaining AI to business people works the same way: lead with the outcome, use their words, and be honest about risks.

💡 In simple words

Forward deployed engineers, PMs and consultants spend much of their time with people who don't care about embeddings. They care about time, money, risk and their customers. Structure updates as: the outcome or decision needed, the evidence (a demo or numbers), the risks and how they're handled, then next steps. Replace jargon with analogies. Show a working prototype early, since it beats ten slides. Listen more than you talk in discovery meetings, and confirm requirements in writing. Be upfront about what AI can't do: trust is your most valuable asset.

🔑 Key terms

Stakeholder
Anyone affected by, or deciding on, the project.
Discovery
Meetings to understand the real problem, users and constraints.
Executive summary
The 3–4 line version: outcome, evidence, risk, ask.

👀 See it in action

text
❌ "We implemented hybrid retrieval with a re-ranker and improved MRR@10 by 0.18."
✅ "The assistant now finds the right policy 9 times out of 10, up from 7.
    Agents save ~4 minutes per ticket (≈ ₹6 lakh/month).
    Risk: rare wrong answers → it always shows its source.
    Decision needed: roll out to 2 more teams?"

Same work, explained as outcome → evidence → risk → ask.

✅ Quick check

What should you lead with when updating a business leader?

  1. Your tech stack
  2. Model architecture details
  3. The business outcome and the decision you need
Show answer

C. The business outcome and the decision you need: Leaders decide on outcomes and risks. Give them that first, and details on request.

📌 Remember

Outcome → evidence → risk → ask. Analogies over jargon. Demos over slides.
