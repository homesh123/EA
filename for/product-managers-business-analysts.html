Best AI roles for product managers, project managers and business analysts

Product managers, project managers and business analysts already do the hardest part of AI product work: understanding users, defining problems and getting teams to deliver. Moving into AI product management means adding AI literacy, hands-on prototyping and the discipline of evaluating AI quality.

Quick answer

For most product managers, project managers and business analysts, the best AI role is AI Product Manager (96% fit for a typical profile), followed by AI Forward Deployed Engineer (53%) and AI Foundations Path (32%). Why it usually fits: you already manage products, projects or requirements; you like deciding what to build and why; your industry knowledge helps you find the right AI use cases. A typical person makes the switch in about 4.2 months at 8 hours of study a week.

  2. AI Forward Deployed Engineer 53% fit

    Deploy AI inside real businesses, hands-on with customers. Strong alternative. About 4.4 months at 8 h/week.

    • You care about business impact
    • Your industry knowledge is a big advantage with clients
    • Your years of experience build client trust

  3. AI Foundations Path 32% fit

    Build the base, then land a junior AI / ML role. Strong alternative. About 5.8 months at 8 h/week.

    • You're early in coding, so a strong foundation is the fastest route
    • You're new to ML, so we start from the basics

Skills you already have that transfer to AI

  • Requirements, prioritisation and delivery — the core of AI product work
  • Knowledge of retail / e-commerce — industry AI roles value it highly
  • 9+ years of experience — judgement, communication and ownership transfer to any AI role
  • Solid SQL — data skills underpin all AI work
  • Strong reasoning — you got 4 of 4 check questions right

Advice for product managers, project managers and business analysts moving into AI

  • Learn how LLMs fail (hallucinations, cost, latency) so you can make good trade-offs, not just write specs.
  • Prototype ideas yourself with prompting and no-code tools before writing a PRD.
  • Every AI feature needs an evaluation plan. Learn to write one, because it's the most common gap in AI PM interviews.

Your first steps towards AI Product Manager

  1. AI Literacy for Decision-Makers (2-3 weeks): How LLMs and ML models work, what they're good and bad at, costs, risks and hallucinations.
  2. Hands-On Prototyping (Low-Code) (2-3 weeks): Build prototypes with prompting, no-code tools and simple scripts so you can test ideas yourself.
