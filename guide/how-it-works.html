How our free AI career consultation works

AI Path Finder recommends an AI role the way a good career advisor would: it looks at what you already do, what you're good at, how you like to work and how much time you have. Unlike a black box, every rule is written down here.

Quick answer

You answer about 20 questions in 5 sections. A 4-question reasoning check validates your self-ratings, and contradictory answers are corrected. Each of 9 AI roles is then scored with weighted, explainable signals, such as your current work, skills, preferred kind of work and interests. You see your best-fit role with a fit percentage and the reasons, a readiness score, skill gaps, transferable strengths and a roadmap dated to your weekly study hours.

1. The questions we ask

About you

  • Which best describes you right now?
  • How many years of work experience do you have?
  • What is (or was) your main area of work?
  • Which AI role are you in today?
  • What is pushing you towards AI right now?

Your skills today

  • Which programming languages can you write code in?
  • How would you rate your Python?
  • How comfortable are you with SQL and data?
  • How much cloud / deployment experience do you have?
  • What have you done with AI / ML so far?
  • How comfortable are you with math (statistics, algebra)?

Quick reasoning check

  • The average of four numbers is 10. Three of them are 8, 9 and 11. What is the fourth?
  • A model is 99% accurate at spotting a disease that only 1% of people have. Is it a good model?
  • An AI chatbot gave correct answers last week. This week, with no code changes, its answers are wrong. What do you check first?
  • A retail client says: "Use AI to stop customers leaving us." What is the best first step?

How you like to work

  • Which kind of workday sounds most like you?
  • Which parts of AI excite you?
  • Which industry do you know best?

Goals & time

  • What do you want to achieve?
  • How many hours a week can you realistically study?
  • By when do you want to be job-ready?

Some questions only appear when relevant. For example, students aren't asked about their current job.

2. How we validate your answers

  • Reasoning check: four short questions (numeric reasoning, data intuition, systems thinking, problem framing) test the abilities people tend to over- or under-rate.
  • Maths calibration: rating your maths as strong but missing the maths question lowers it to "okay". Rating it as weak but solving the question raises it slightly.
  • Consistency rules: for example, listing Python as a language but rating it "none" is treated as basic Python. Claiming model training without any programming language is treated as having used AI tools.
  • Every adjustment is shown to you on the results page, with the reason.

3. How each AI role is scored

Each role has a set of weighted signals. A role's fit percentage is its score compared with a near-ideal profile for that role. The strongest signals for each role are below (+ raises the score, − lowers it). These are the same sentences you see as reasons on your results.

4. Readiness, skill gaps and timeline

Each role lists target levels for Python, SQL, cloud, hands-on AI and maths. Readiness is how much of that you already have, and anything below target becomes a skill gap with advice. Roadmap steps that cover skills you already have are marked fast-track and shortened to a quick revision. The remaining study hours are spread over your weekly hours to give real dates, which are checked against your target date.

5. Limits of the recommendation

  • It relies on your self-ratings. The reasoning check reduces this problem but doesn't remove it.
  • Salary ranges are indicative, drawn from public sources (Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, AmbitionBox and 2026 salary guides) that often disagree.
  • It is guidance to focus your learning, not a job guarantee. Use the top 2–3 matches as a shortlist and validate with a small project, as described in how to choose the right AI role.

Frequently asked questions

Is the AI career consultation really free?

Yes. It runs entirely in your browser, needs no sign-up, and your answers are never sent to a server.

How accurate is the AI role recommendation?

It is a structured, transparent starting point, not a guarantee. We checked it against realistic profiles (for example backend developers, QA testers, DevOps engineers, accountants and freshers) to confirm it recommends what an experienced career advisor would. It relies on honest self-ratings, which is why it includes a short reasoning check.

Does a human review my answers?

No. The recommendation is calculated by transparent rules described on this page, so you can see exactly why a role was recommended.
